# AI Marketing Playbook — Quietforge Dual-Agent Operations

This playbook is an operational guide for LinkedIn and B2B growth work. It is
adapted to this repository's canon; it is not a claim that LinkedIn has published
an official “360 Brew” algorithm specification. Platform behaviour changes, so
recommendations are hypotheses to test against account data.

## 1. Operating model

### Two brands, two jobs

- **Quietforge** (`quietforge.flexgrafik.nl`): Conversion Systems Architect for
  Dutch SMBs, small BVs, professional services, and an investor bridge.
- **FlexGrafik** (`flexgrafik.nl`): the live operating portfolio and production
  proof. Use it to demonstrate systems, not to blur the Quietforge offer.

Use the funnel:

```text
Pain recognition → working proof → safety → commercial clarity
→ Automation Map → scoped build or honest no-build recommendation
```

Primary question: **Can this person design and deploy a system that improves my
business?**

### Evidence discipline

Every claim gets one label:

- **PROVEN** — supported by a traceable measurement, artefact, or live behaviour.
- **DEMO** — a labelled demonstration or fixture.
- **PLANNED** — intended, not yet live.

Never invent MRR, leads, conversion rates, reach, engagement, uptime, customers,
or platform penalties. If evidence is missing, say what would verify it.

## 2. LinkedIn Profile Audit

### Inputs

Request, when available: profile URL or copied profile text, target ICP, offer,
geography/language, proof assets, current CTA, 5–10 representative posts, and
analytics for the last 30–90 days. Do not request passwords or private credentials.

### Procedure

1. **Positioning scan** — identify who the person helps, painful problem, mechanism,
   outcome, differentiation, proof, and next step. Flag vague labels such as
   “AI expert” or “digital transformation”.
2. **Semantic search scan** — extract the buyer's real vocabulary and check its
   natural presence in headline, About, experience, Featured, skills, and recent
   posts. Score relevance and clarity; do not keyword-stuff.
3. **Conversion path scan** — check whether a stranger can understand the offer in
   five seconds, see proof, trust the operator, and take one next step. Check the
   Featured section for a coherent sequence rather than a link dump.
4. **Proof scan** — map each claim to PROVEN/DEMO/PLANNED and identify missing
   evidence, weak specificity, or audience mismatch.
5. **Content scan** — review hooks, problem specificity, original insight,
   structure, useful takeaway, conversation quality, saves, dwell-time proxies,
   profile views, qualified replies, and outbound clicks. Treat platform signals
   as directional, not as a guaranteed formula.
6. **Trust and safety scan** — assess human oversight, privacy, expectations,
   delivery boundaries, and whether copy overclaims autonomy.
7. **Prioritisation** — deliver P0 (blocks comprehension or trust), P1 (limits
   qualified conversion), and P2 (polish/experiments). Give before/after copy for
   the first three fixes.

### Audit output

```text

Current positioning:
P0/P1/P2 findings:
Semantic keyword map:
Proof inventory:
Recommended headline:
Recommended About structure:

Measurement plan:
Commander decision:
```

## 3. Competitor Analysis

### Scope

Analyse direct alternatives (automation consultants, systems architects, productised
service providers) and indirect alternatives (DIY tools, agencies, internal hires).
Use public sources only. Record URL, date checked, audience, offer, price signal,
claim, proof type, CTA, content pattern, and observable engagement.

### Comparison dimensions

1. Category and language used.
2. ICP and pain selected.
3. Promise and mechanism.
4. Proof quality and transparency.
5. Commercial entry point and risk reversal.
6. Content themes, hooks, formats, and publishing cadence.
7. Search terms and semantic associations.
8. CTA path and friction.
9. Governance, privacy, and human-control signals.
10. White space Quietforge can own without copying.

Do not infer revenue, customer satisfaction, or algorithm preference from public
engagement alone. Distinguish observation, interpretation, and hypothesis.

### Deliverable

Produce a short battlecard per competitor, a category pattern summary, a white-space
thesis, and three differentiated experiments. Recommend competing on clarity,
governed delivery, proof, and a smaller credible first step—not on unsupported
superlatives.

## 4. B2B Positioning

Use this sentence structure:

> Quietforge helps **[specific ICP]** fix **[expensive business leak]** through
> **[governed conversion/operations system]**, so they can **[measurable or
> observable effect]** without **[key risk or anti-positioning]**.

Build the message house:

- **Category:** Conversion Systems Architect.
- **Buyer:** NL SMB/small BV or professional service operator.
- **Pain:** manual quotes, inbox chaos, weak qualification, or a site that does not
  become pipeline.
- **Mechanism:** supervised systems that qualify, route, and prepare work while the
  human remains in control.
- **Proof:** traceable live systems, artefacts, walkthroughs, and honest status.
- **Entry offer:** Automation Map, €290 credited toward a first build.
- **Anti-positioning:** not a web designer, chatbot builder, or generic agency.

For each offer, write:

```text
Problem → System → Effect → Proof → Safety → Price → CTA
```

Use AIDA, PAS, or BAB intentionally:

- **AIDA** for a concise profile or broad awareness post.
- **PAS** when the buyer already feels the operational leak.
- **BAB** for a case study, transformation story, or build-in-public post.

## 5. LinkedIn content and experiment protocol

Default post shape:

```text
Specific hook
↓
Observed problem or tension
↓
Mechanism / system decision
↓
Proof or clearly labelled demo
↓
Useful takeaway worth saving
↓
One question or next step
```

Rules:

- No hashtag clouds; default to zero hashtags.
- Prefer native content and useful specificity over engagement bait.
- Design for rereads and saves; use short lines, concrete examples, and a durable
  takeaway to support reading time.
- External links may reduce distribution in some contexts, but this is a testable
  hypothesis, not a universal penalty. If a link is needed, use the first comment or
  Featured where appropriate and add UTMs.
- Test one variable at a time: hook, format, proof density, CTA, or link placement.
- Use a 2–4 week timebox and define a baseline before judging a result.
- Primary outcomes: qualified replies, profile actions, booked Map/intake events,
  and attributable sessions. Saves and dwell-time proxies are supporting signals.

## 6. Growth operating loop

```text
Instrument → publish/test → inspect → learn → propose → Commander decides → act
```

Every experiment brief must state:

- hypothesis and audience;
- asset and channel;
- CTA and destination;
- UTM/event plan;
- baseline and success signal;
- timebox;
- stop/continue rule;
- human approval required.

Tool-first rule: configure and verify the measurement or operating tool before
scaling live distribution. Test publishes are proof-only and must be deleted unless
the Commander explicitly unlocks live publication.
