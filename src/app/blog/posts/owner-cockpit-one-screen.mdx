Most business dashboards fail the same way. They show thirty numbers, and none of them tells you what to do on Monday morning. So the owner closes the tab and runs the company from memory again.

A cockpit is different from a dashboard. A dashboard displays data. A cockpit is built around one question only: what needs a decision this week?

## One screen, one truth

In most small companies, the truth about the business lives in four places: leads in the CRM, orders in a spreadsheet, margins in the accountant’s report, and cash in the banking app. Reconciling them is a manual job, and by the time it is done the numbers are a week old.

The owner cockpit pulls those into one screen with five to eight numbers, no more:

- **Leads:** how many came in, how many are stuck.
- **Orders:** what was won, what is at risk.
- **Margin:** what the last batch of work actually earned after costs.
- **Cash:** what is coming in, what is going out, when.

Every number traces back to one source system. No averaging, no manual exports, no two versions of the truth.

## The weekly brief: a draft, not a demand

Numbers alone still do not make decisions. So the cockpit prepares a weekly brief: what changed since last week, what is off track, and what it proposes to do about it.

The brief is a draft. It proposes — for example, “three leads have had no follow-up for six days; shall I draft follow-ups?” — and you approve or dismiss each item with one click. The system never acts on its own. That is the same rule everywhere: the model proposes, policy decides, you click.

## No vanity dashboards

A metric earns its place on the screen only if it changes a decision. If a number is there because it looks impressive — page views, follower counts, activity graphs — it does not belong in a cockpit. Vanity metrics flatter; they do not steer.

The result is quiet: one screen, reviewed once a week, with every line pointing at an action. That is what an [owner cockpit](/systems/owner-cockpit/) looks like when it is done — not more data, but fewer questions.
